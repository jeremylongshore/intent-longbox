## 2. Decision A — The asset inventory, as rows with an owner, a class, a home and a reader/writer matrix

### 2.1 The rule

> **Every data class Longbox holds or transmits is a row in this table. **B13 is the amendment that changes the shape of the map, and it is worth stating why the draft missed it.** The draft drew B6 as one boundary because one TCP connection carries both directions. **A trust boundary is not a connection; it is a direction of trust**, and the two directions have opposite properties: outbound, the server is authenticated (`x-api-key`) and controls the payload; inbound, nothing is authenticated and the far side controls the payload. Splitting them makes four cells sayable that the merged row could not hold:

- **Spoofing — `none`.** No host pinning, no certificate pin, no response signature. `base_url` is a column (`registry.ts:63`, `:72`), so whoever writes that column chooses who answers.
- **Tampering — type-only.** `shared.ts:19-27` validates three nullable strings and a number. **No length bound, no character class, no null-rate check.**
- **Information disclosure — real and two-hop.** The raw response is stored on `llm_rerank` (A5) and its derived `reasons` are echoed to the caller inside the `409 CONTRADICTION_BLOCKS_ONE_TAP` details (`sessionApi.ts:422-425`). A response from a host of the attacker's choosing is therefore both persisted and reflected.
- **Elevation of privilege — the response sets its own band.** `identify.ts:134` reads `result.confidence` from the response and hands it to `assignBand`. **The far side of an unauthenticated inbound boundary decides whether a human is asked (§5 A8, R-3).**

> **E12, E15 and R-3 are not three findings. They are one exposure with three names, and it becomes constructible the moment any surface writes `base_url`: choose the host, choose the response, choose the band, and the one-tap confirm is yours.** That is the sentence E03-B05 and E03-B02/B03 must read before either ships a write surface.

**Three properties of this list are the decision, not the drawing.**

- **B5 is a boundary in its own right and is not the read side of B4.** Writing a file to local disk is a process-internal act; *publishing* that file to the internet is a different principal receiving a different asset. Collapsing them is how a public mount survives a review — because "the server writes photos to disk" reads as unremarkable and "the server publishes every shop's photos" reads as the defect it is.
- **B10 exists on this list although the surface does not.** A boundary that will exist, whose shape is decided before it is built, is cheaper than one discovered by its first forged request. 042 §2.2 made the same argument about versioning: *"the version is not for today's client."*
- **B6 is the only boundary where a database row decides who the far side is.** That is E12, and it is why §4 scores B6 and B13 as it does.
- **One thing that is deliberately NOT a boundary, recorded at ratification so nobody adds it later.** `demos.intentsolutions.io/longbox/` serves the rendered 045 diagram as a static file from the dev box's Caddy. **It is a picture of this system and not a part of it**: it holds no shop data, reaches no Longbox route, shares no origin, credential or database, and its compromise would misinform a reader rather than reach an asset. It gets no `Bn` because a boundary with nothing on the far side to protect trains readers to discount the numbering.

## 4. Decision C — STRIDE, per boundary, reporting the control that exists today

### 4.1 The rule

> **Every boundary gets a STRIDE row. Each cell names the control that exists TODAY with its `file:line`, or the word `none`. A control that is decided but unbuilt goes in the "decided" column with its bead. Likelihood and impact are words — `low` / `moderate` / `high` — and never numbers (018 A3, 019 §2).**

The reason for the `file:line`-or-`none` rule is 042 §0's and 043 A3's, in a different subject: *"An instruction is not an invariant. It is the thing an invariant exists to replace."* A STRIDE cell that says *"validated at the boundary"* with no citation is an instruction.

**Amended at ratification (K-1, `martin-kleppmann-reviewer`): every non-`none` cell carries a sub-label saying what KIND of control it is.**

| Sub-label | Meaning | Why it is worth a word |
|---|---|---|
| **structural** | The property holds because the system cannot express its violation — a constraint, a role that owns nothing, a hardcoded literal | Survives a bug, a refactor and a careless engineer |
| **mechanism** | Code that runs and can refuse — a check, a validator, a boot assertion, a timer | Survives a careless engineer; does not survive being deleted |
| **absence-noted** | Nothing protects this; it is currently unreachable because the surface that would reach it has not been built | **Not a control.** It is a fact about scope with an expiry date nobody has set |

The lens's objection was exact: *"A cell reading `outbound x-api-key` and a cell reading `ENABLE ALWAYS triggers` are both non-empty and they are not the same kind of promise. Without the distinction the table's density is its own argument, and the reader counts non-empty cells."* **The sub-labels also make the record's real shape legible in one pass: the structural cells cluster in B2 and B3, and every outward-facing boundary that is non-empty is `mechanism` or `absence-noted`.**

**Three boundaries carry a `none` in the Spoofing column — B1, B5 and B10 — and they are the three that face outward.** Every inward-facing boundary (B2, B3, B11) has a working control, and two of them fail closed. **That is not a coincidence and it is not a criticism of the work done so far**: the estate built the boundaries that protect the log from the application because 041 §9 asked it to, and nobody has yet been asked to build the boundary that protects the application from a caller. **One boundary is anomalous, and it is B6.** It is the only boundary where an authenticated outbound channel's **destination** is data rather than configuration. Every other outbound boundary is pinned by an environment variable or a constant; B6's host comes from a database column that the application role can write (E12). **A boundary whose far side is a value is not a boundary; it is a parameter.**

## 6. Decision E — The ranked gap list, and where each gap closes

### 6.1 The correction this section has to make first

The bead's framing, and the natural reading of 019, is *"what must close before Pilot A versus before G2"*. **That ordering is backwards, and 034 §3.2's go-live posture (`034:414-421`) settles it**: 019 §5 puts **T24 in CI at G2** and places **Pilot A behind G3**. 019 §3.0's eight safety controls are *"before the first live item"* and are owned at **G2**. Stating it this way removes the reading that a control may be deferred *"to Pilot A"* as though that were later-but-still-early. It is later **and** past the gate that was supposed to catch it.

### 6.3 The ordering argument, which is not the same as the list

**Rank 1 is thirteen rows after ratification, and the count moved for reasons worth stating.** G-27 joined it (R-3, a live one-tap bypass), A17 folded into G-9 rather than taking a row of its own (R-5, same owner and same fix), and **G-4 stopped being a subsystem and became a deletion** (Q5) — so the list got one longer and materially cheaper. **Four of the thirteen are `hours` and one of those removes code.** That is the honest answer to Q4: the gate is not too long, it is badly ordered, and the fix is to do the cheap rows first rather than to move the expensive ones out of sight.

Twelve rank-1 rows was more than one bead's worth of work, thirteen still is, and the order within them is a decision. **G-7 goes first** because 019 marks it a G1 blocker, which is earlier than every other line here, and because it costs one column and one gate rather than a subsystem. **G-1 goes next and unblocks five others** — G-2, G-4 (a private route needs a caller), G-10 (a write surface needs an owner), G-11 and G-20 all become expressible only once a caller has a name. **G-3 goes early rather than last**, reversing the ordering 034 implied, because its blocker is discharged (E8) and because RLS is the layer that survives a handler bug: 034 §3.4 (`034:447`) — *"the contract fails to a code review, the runtime assertion fails to a bug, and RLS fails to a database misconfiguration."* **G-4, G-5 and G-6 ship together** because they are one path and shipping the private mount without byte validation moves the defect rather than closing it. **Amended by the Q5 ruling: G-4's deletion may ship alone and before all of them**, because deleting a mount cannot move a defect — it removes a reader, and the bytes it stops publishing are the same bytes G-5 and G-6 will later validate and strip.

**And one correction to the draft's account of why G-3 goes early (K-5, `martin-kleppmann-reviewer`).** The draft said RLS is unblocked because 029 §12's request transaction shipped. **That understates it: the transaction is not merely available, it is already load-bearing on the request path.** `runIdempotent` wraps every mutating request in `withTransaction` (`idempotency.ts:258`), so the `BEGIN`…`COMMIT` that `SET LOCAL` needs is not a facility E03-B04 must adopt — it is the envelope every write already runs inside. **The lens's point was that "unblocked" and "the substrate is in production use" are different claims, and only the second one makes the ordering argument.** The failure mode E03-B04's property tests must cover is therefore concrete and named: `SET LOCAL` on a pooled connection outside a transaction leaks into the next request that borrows it, which is why the context is set inside `withTransaction` and never per-request.

### 7.3 The finding

**019 T31 (`019:114`) names six surfaces and two cadences. CI covers the repository and one cadence — and "the repository" means, precisely, *default rules minus one permanently exempted commit and one unasserted naming convention* (R-1).**

**The scope sentence corrected.** The draft said the scan runs on gitleaks' defaults *because no rule file exists*. `.gitleaks.toml` exists (E26), and what it does is **narrow** the defaults twice: `commits = ["192d632…"]` exempts an entire commit permanently, and a `test-[a-z0-9-]+(-key|-token|-secret)…` regex exempts anything matching a naming convention that **no test asserts** — so a real credential named to match is invisible to the scan. Both narrowings are defensible as written and neither is bounded, which is G-9's content.

**And a scope limit the draft did not state at all: gitleaks sees the git tree and history, so gitignored working trees are outside it by construction.** `.gitignore:60` ignores `.claude/worktrees/`, where agent sessions hold full checkouts. Nothing decrypted has ever been written there and nothing should be; **the point is that if it were, no scan in this repository would find it** — which is a reason the T31 surfaces that are not the repository cannot be deferred to the repository scan.

> *"secret-scan hits in DB columns, logs, error payloads, LLM prompts, bead notes, 006 rows, per CI run **and nightly**; BYOK onboarding never transits keys by email or chat"* — **non-waivable, `any → K1`.**

Of the six: **DB columns** — no scan; the honest partial defence is that `shop_credentials` holds names by construction (E11) and every other column is domain data. **Amended at ratification (R-5): that defence has a hole with a command attached.** `pnpm fixture:schema` runs `pg_dump` with no `--schema-only` and no table filter against `MIGRATE_DATABASE_URL` and writes inside the repository (`makeSchemaFixture.ts:123-127`, §5 A17) — **so the uncovered DB-columns surface has a one-command path into the covered repository surface, and the row most dangerous to carry across it is the one the scanner is structurally least able to recognise**, because a `key_ref` is a variable name and not a credential shape. It is one flag to fix. **Logs** — no scan; the partial defence is `app.ts:45-48`'s redaction and 042 §4.3's rule that the server emits no prose. **Error payloads** — no scan; `http/errors.ts:70` drops the thrown message. **LLM prompts** — no scan, and this is the one with no partial defence at all: `buildUserText` (`shared.ts:52-55`) will interpolate candidate metadata as soon as E06 populates it. **Bead notes and 006 rows** — no scan; the convention is the whole control.

> **Decision: T31's scope is repository-plus-five-more, the CI job is the repository half, and the other five plus the nightly cadence are E03-B05's with a detector rather than a convention. This record does not build it and does not let the CI job be mistaken for it.**

**And one thing the inventory settles that was not obvious.** `LLM_BASE_URL` + `LLM_API_KEY` **win over every per-shop credential** (`registry.ts:44-55`). A gateway override is therefore a single environment pair that redirects every shop's vision traffic — the same shape as E12 with a different write surface. It belongs on E03-B05's list beside `key_ref`, and §11 I5 asserts it is startup-validated rather than silently accepted.

## 8. Decision G — The standing-review rule

### 8.1 The rule

> **This record is redrawn when a boundary changes, never on a calendar. A boundary changes when: a route is added outside the tenant prefix; a new outbound host is contacted; a new asset class is stored; a principal is added to §2.3's matrix; or an allowlist row's `kind` changes. The bead that makes such a change amends the affected §2/§3 row in the same PR, exactly as 042 A4 requires the identify DTO and its client to land together.**

### 8.2 Why not a review cadence

045 §Change control uses the same trigger-based rule (*"Redraw when a route is added or moved, a lane changes owner, or a planned step becomes live"*), and the reason generalises. **A calendar review of a threat model produces a document that was accurate on one day per quarter.** A trigger-based rule produces one that is accurate whenever the thing it describes has stopped moving — and the triggers above are exactly the events the route walk (`tests/contract/route-table-scoping.test.ts`), the architecture gate (`pnpm arch`) and the declared-list assertion already detect. **The mechanism exists; this rule points it at this document.**

**One consequence, stated because it is a cost.** Every E03 bead will touch this record, which makes it a merge-contention point. The alternative — a threat model that is written once and cited forever — is the artifact 013's audit found everywhere and named as the failure mode. **A record that is expensive to keep true is better than one that is cheap to keep stale.**

## 10. Alternatives considered

**A1 — Score risk numerically (CVSS, or a likelihood×impact product).** *Rejected.* `SECURITY.md` already carries a CVSS band table for *reported* vulnerabilities, and that is the right place for it: a reporter's severity is an input from outside. **A number attached to a threat this project has not measured is exactly what 019 §2 and 018 A3 forbid**, and the failure mode is concrete — a "7.5" becomes a fact in a later artifact and nobody can reconstruct what it was derived from. Words on a three-value scale carry the same ordering and cannot be averaged into a claim.

**A2 — One combined table of assets, flows and threats.** *Rejected.* It is the shape most threat models take and it collapses under its own width: an asset has a class and a retention answer, a boundary has two sides and a protocol, a threat has a likelihood and a detector, and forcing them into one row means every cell is empty for two-thirds of the rows. The three tables cite each other by number, which is what makes §6's ranking checkable.

**A3 — Defer the threat model until authentication exists**, on the ground that most of it is *"there is no control"*. *Rejected, and it is the alternative a reasonable reader reaches for.* 014 §8 makes E03-B01 the dependency of every other E03 bead (`014:394-402`), and the reason is visible in this document: **the ordering in §6 is the deliverable**, and it cannot be derived after the fact by whoever happens to build authentication first. E03-B04's discharged blocker (E8) is the worked example — waiting would have meant building RLS last because a ratified record said it was blocked, three days after the blocker was removed.

**A4 — Treat B5 (the public mount) as part of B4 (the disk write).** *Rejected*, §3.3. Collapsing them is how a public mount survives review.

**A5 — Write the statutory analysis here**, since the retention windows, the consent path and the deletion right are all legal constructs. *Rejected*, §0. 041 §8.7(d) set the practice and the reason holds: a security record that reasons about a statute it has not been advised on is producing a legal opinion. The four questions go to the counsel batch with their technical context attached.

**A6 — Publish a redacted version as a trust artifact.** *Rejected.* 021 retires *"secure"* until E03-B10's independent review (`021:57`), 021 B16 caps every `control` and `contract` line at commitment, and this record's entire content is the list of things that are not true yet. **There is no redaction of this document that is both honest and reassuring**, which is the correct answer for a system with thirteen rank-1 gaps.

### 13.1 The questions as they were put

Seven, and the first four are the ones a lens should be able to move.

1. **Is §9.2's `key_ref` / `base_url` constraint a `v1` change or E03-B05's?** E12 is unreachable today (E13) and the constraint is two validators. The case for now: it is cheap, and the surface that exposes it is the next bead. The case for later: a namespace rule written before the vault exists may be the wrong namespace rule, and 043 A2's lesson is that a design fixed against zero running code is unfalsifiable. **Which failure is recoverable?**
2. **Can A7 (key-ref confusion between shops) have a detector at all, or only a constraint?** T31's instrument is a secret *scan*, which by construction cannot see a correctly-named variable resolved by the wrong shop. If the answer is "constraint only", then T31 does not cover A7 and something else must — and 019 standing amendment 2 says a non-waivable line without a detector is a waiver by omission. **Is this a gap in T31's coverage that needs a 019 amend-by-row, or is the constraint the detector?**
3. **A1 and A2 — the employee who records a wrong condition or a wrong issue — have no detector and, under 022 P3 and 019 T35, may not have a per-operator one.** 022 P1 already says a wrong call is a system defect and never an employee fault, and 039 §5 makes that a contractual promise. **Is "no detector, by design, because the only detector would be the forbidden surface" the correct answer — or is there an aggregate instrument (a batch-level dispute rate, an owner-review signal) that catches the pattern without naming a person?**
4. **Is §6.2's rank-1 list the right cut, and is twelve too many to be a gate?** A gate that lists twelve blockers is a gate that will be argued down. **Would a smaller rank-1 — say G-1, G-2, G-4, G-7 — with the rest at G3 be more honest, or would it be the deferral this record exists to prevent?**
5. **Should B5 (the public mount) be closed by deletion rather than by replacement?** E28 shows the mount is not actually serving its downstream purpose — Shopify gets relative paths and cannot fetch them. So removing the mount today breaks the phone client's image preview and nothing else. **Is "delete the mount now, add signed URLs at E03-B07" a smaller change than "wait for E03-B07"?**
6. **Does §8's trigger-based review rule survive contact with twelve beads all touching this document?** 042 A4's same-PR constraint worked for two files. **Is there a version of §8 that keeps the record true without making it a bottleneck — a generated section, perhaps, with I15 as the assertion?**
