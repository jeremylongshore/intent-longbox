## 1. What this record decides

**Six rulings.** §4.3 (A5 enforced at registration) and §4.5 (047 A8's paired-edit gate extended to the manifests) are consequences of ruling 2, not independent decisions; the change log's "eight" counts them separately, and this section does not.

1. **The manifest ships eleven fields**, and every one of them names its consumer. §3. **The arithmetic, since v1.0.0 asserted it without showing it:** 030 §5.1's interface declares **ten** slots (`vertical`, `packVersion`, `displayName`, `identitySchema`, `normalizedSignature`, `captureRecipe`, `conditionSchema`, `providerCrosswalk`, `valuationNormalization`, `prohibitedClaims`), and its table names **two** more as RESERVED, which is where a "fourteen" would come from if the reserved names were counted as fields and the two column-backed additions ignored. Ten − `normalizedSignature` + `verticalCode` + `signatureFnRef` = **eleven**. **Five things are omitted**, not four: `normalizedSignature`, `providerCrosswalk[].normalize`, `providerCrosswalk[].capabilities`, and the two reserved names.
2. **`certify()` is a pure gate**, run before a manifest can become a row. §4.
3. **The authoritative vertical codes are `cmc`, `spc` and `tcg`.** §5.
4. **One manifest per registered vertical** (implementing 049's F3 ruling), and **`packRegistry.ts` stays a separate module** (049 F2). §6.
5. **A pack is registered by an operator script under `MIGRATE_DATABASE_URL`**, which **refuses the application role by a check** — not by a migration, and not by a seed at boot. §7.
6. **The card grade ladder drops MINT and GEM**, and certification refuses either band in any vertical. §8.

## 3. Decision A — The manifest's field list, under Fowler F1

030 A3 trimmed the sketched interface to *"the fields the comic pack populates"* and made the rule explicit: **a manifest field is added when a pack needs it, not before.** The bead restates it harder — *a manifest field that nothing exercises is not declared* — and this section is the audit that rule forces. **Every field ships with a named consumer, and the FIVE omissions are named too** (v1.0.0 said four while its own table listed five rows covering five names — `normalizedSignature`, `providerCrosswalk[].normalize`, `providerCrosswalk[].capabilities`, `listingTemplate`, `evalSlices`).

| Field | Consumer, named |

| `displayName` | **ONE consumer, and it is three `console.log` lines**: `scripts/register-pack.ts:171` (already-registered), `:180` (dry run) and `:206` (registered). **The thinnest consumer in the manifest, and it is the only field with no column behind it** — 030 §5.2's sketch put `display_name` on `vertical_pack` (`030:327`), and `migrations/016_catalog_core.sql:82-91` **dropped it**, so this string lives only inside `manifest jsonb`. **Two fields were ADDED beyond §5.1's interface, and neither is an amendment.** `verticalCode` and `signatureFnRef` are columns 030 §5.2 already specified on `vertical_pack` / `vertical_pack_version`; putting them in the manifest is what lets ONE artifact fill the row.

**And `packVersion` is an INTEGER, not semver.** §5.1's comment says semver; `migrations/016_catalog_core.sql:103` says `pack_version integer NOT NULL CHECK (pack_version > 0)`. The shipped column wins. A manifest that could not be written into the row it exists to fill would be a contract with itself.

## 4. Decision B — Certification

**What certification is for, in one sentence.** A `vertical_pack_version` row governs how every later `collectible_definition` and `edition` in that vertical is read, the row can never be updated, and so a manifest that is wrong is wrong FOREVER for every row that cites it. Certification refuses, before the write, what could not be repaired after it.

### 4.1 The refusals, by code

**The count is a command, not a claim.** `/usr/bin/grep -c 'code: "' src/catalog/packManifest.ts` → **27** emission sites; `/usr/bin/grep -o 'code: "[A-Z_]*"' src/catalog/packManifest.ts | sort -u | wc -l` → **27** distinct codes. v1.0.0 called this table *"the ten refusals"*, which was a description of the table's row count rather than of the module, and it omitted five codes: `PACK_SCHEMA_NOT_AN_OBJECT`, `DUPLICATE_GRADE_LABEL`, `DUPLICATE_SOURCE`, and the two `VERSION_*` codes that `assertVersionMoved` raises (§4.3). All five are below.

| Code | Refuses | Authority |

### 4.2 The one that is worth reading twice: signature positions are PROVED, not declared

The manifest flags each field `inDefinitionSignature` and `inEditionSignature`. Certification does not take the flags on trust and does not compare them against a second copy of the field list — **there is no list to compare with, because the signature is a function.** So it asks the function: build a payload from the declared fields, perturb one field, and assert the signature moves **if and only if** the manifest says that field is in that key.

**The four cases that decide it** are in `tests/pack-manifest.test.ts:147-181`: a field declared in the edition key that the function ignores (`PHANTOM_SIGNATURE_POSITION`, on the comic `cover`), a field that moves the key while the manifest says it does not (`UNDECLARED_SIGNATURE_POSITION`, on `variant`), an edition field claiming a position in the WORK's key (`EDITION_FIELD_IN_WORK_KEY`, on `issue`), and a work-key claim the definition signature does not honour (`PHANTOM_SIGNATURE_POSITION` again, on `publisher`).

**⚠ AND THE CLAIM ABOUT 030 §5.4's PRE-v1.3.0 CARD SKETCH IS A COUNTERFACTUAL, NOT A RUN.** That sketch listed `parallel` as a card edition attribute and left it out of the key, so a Silver Prizm #12 and a base #12 signed identically — the defect 049 §5.2 found by reading. **Nothing here re-runs that sketch**; what is tested is its SHAPE, by the comic `cover` case, which is the same defect with different field names: an attribute declared as identity that the key cannot see. Saying "this test would have failed on `parallel`" is a claim about the shape being the same, and it is worth stating that way rather than as evidence, because 018 §2 A3's rule — a threshold is not evidence that it is met — reads the same for a mechanism.

The definition side gets the same treatment against `definitionSignature`, and an EDITION field claiming a position in the WORK's key is refused outright: the work's key is computed from the definition's attributes alone, so a printing-level fact in it would split one work into one work per printing.

### 4.3 030 A5, enforced where it can be

A5 makes the pack version **single-rate**: schema, signature function, capture recipe, condition schema, crosswalk, valuation and prohibited claims move together under one number. That is not checkable from a manifest alone — *"the version moved"* is a statement about two versions — so `assertVersionMoved(previous, next)` takes the row already on disk and the registration script is where it runs:

- content changed and the version did not rise → **refused**;
- content unchanged and the version moved → **refused** (a version row that changes nothing splits the rows citing it across two answers that are the same answer);
- both moved together, or neither → allowed.

**The comparison excludes `packVersion` itself**, and that is the whole rule: comparing the whole manifest would make every bump look like a change, so a bump that changed nothing else would pass — and a bump is exactly what a careless author reaches for.

### 4.4 `prohibitedClaims` are 021 ROW IDS, never copy

021 is the registry of what may and may not be said. A manifest holding the SENTENCES would be a second copy of that registry, drifting the day 021 takes an amend-by-a-row — which it did on 2026-09-04, when B17–B25 arrived from 037. **A pointer cannot drift.**

`UNIVERSAL_PROHIBITED_CLAIMS` is the subset every pack must carry whatever it sells: **B14** (listing provenance), **B17** (a number as a Longbox grade), **B18** ("Longbox grade"), **B19** ("AI" near condition), **B20** (borrowed certification words), **B22** (a range beside a slab's number), **B24** ("Pressed" as a defect option) and **B25** (adjacency and naming). A pack that omitted one would be a vertical in which a non-waivable line (019 T7, locked decision 5) had quietly been switched off.

### 4.5 The paired-edit gate follows the field list into the manifests

047 A8 forbids `identityKey` and the catalog's identity field lists from merging, by two mechanisms: no shared import, and no PR that edits both sides without a `000-docs/006` row. Its subject set is `CATALOG_IDENTITY_FILES` in `scripts/architectureRules.ts:555-572`, and it grew twice — `comicIdentity.ts` at E04-B02, `cardIdentity.ts` at E04-B03, each time because the new file held a field list.

**A manifest is a field list too, and it is the one a future pack author writes.** `identitySchema.definition` and `.edition` name every field and flag which of them sit in which key, so a diff that edited a manifest's declarations beside `identityKey` is exactly the paired edit A8 forbids — the same edit the E04-B03 row anticipated, wearing a different file extension. So **`src/catalog/comicManifest.ts` and `src/catalog/cardManifests.ts` join the subject set**, and the Architecture gate (044 §6 makes it a required check; 044 §8 is the rule inventory it enforces) fails a PR touching a manifest and `identityKey` together without a 006 row.

It costs nothing today — neither manifest shares an import with `src/services/confirmationOutcome.ts`, and `pnpm arch` is green — and a subject set that grew a member without growing this row would have passed while watching the wrong file, which is the sentence the E04-B03 row already wrote for its own case.

## 5. Decision C — The vertical codes: `cmc`, `spc`, `tcg`

049 §8's row reads, whole and unspliced: **"It does not assign vertical CODES.** `spc` and `tcg` appear in a test fixture; the authoritative assignment is a `vertical_pack` row, and E04-B04 makes it (047 §3.3: assigned in the pack row, never invented per LCID)." (`049:241`. v1.0.0 quoted it as one sentence running across the bolded heading, which it is not.)

It is made here, in the manifests: **`cmc`, `spc`, `tcg`**.

**⚠ AND ONE HALF OF THAT SENTENCE WAS A DRAFTING SLIP, CORRECTED BY ASSIGNMENT RATHER THAN BY AMENDING 049.** `tcg` did NOT appear in a test fixture — it appeared nowhere in the tree as a code (E4: `git grep -o '"tcg"' 6589bb0 -- src scripts tests migrations | wc -l` → **0**; the string occurs only inside `tcg-card`, `TCG_CARD_VERTICAL` and `tcgplayer`). `cmc` (13 occurrences) and `spc` (1) were real fixture literals. The slip cost nothing and needs no amend-by-a-row, because 049 §8's operative clause is that **this bead assigns the codes**, and assigning `tcg` here makes the sentence true going forward. Recording it is cheaper than leaving a future reader to grep for a fixture that never existed.

**Why the fixtures' values rather than better ones.** 047 §3.1 puts the code inside every LCID minted in the vertical and 047 §2 rules that *"the prefix may encode a fact if and only if that fact can never be corrected"* — so the code is a one-way door the moment a row exists. Choosing differently would have orphaned two live fixtures to gain nothing, and the values are already the readable abbreviations. **`vertical_code` is UNIQUE in the DDL and the contract test asserts the three are distinct**, because two verticals sharing a code would be two verticals whose identifiers are indistinguishable forever.

**The registration script refuses to re-register a vertical under a different code**, with the retire-and-re-mint sentence 049 §4 spells out, rather than silently leaving the old code in place — proved on a real database.

## 6. Decision D — Two questions 049's cannon handed this bead

**⚠ ONE OF THEM WAS ALREADY RULED, AND THIS RECORD IMPLEMENTS THE RULING RATHER THAN TAKING IT.** 049 §11.1's *"Ruling on F3 (acting head)"* (`049:310`) says in terms: *"The decision stands"* — two verticals — and prices both directions of the one-way door, closing with *"an unequal pair of one-way doors is chosen by its worse side, not by its likelier one."* What 049 §10 handed E04-B04 was the manifest-shaped consequence of that standing ruling, not the ruling itself. v1.0.0 wrote §6 as though this record decided F3; it does not, and §1's ruling 4 now says "implementing".

**F3 — may a `card` shape carry ONE manifest with a discriminator, rather than one per registered vertical?** **No: one manifest per registered vertical, which is the standing ruling expressed in the row.** A manifest is what a `vertical_pack_version` row holds, and there is one row per vertical because `vertical` is that table's foreign key. A single manifest carrying both would have to be stored twice anyway, once per row, with a discriminator saying which half is live — two rows containing the same document that disagree about which half of themselves applies. **And Fowler's dissent is answered rather than dismissed:** the duplication he priced at *"eleven lines in `packRegistry.ts` and one extra Zod object"* is, here, one shared `cardShape` object and two ten-line literals in ONE file — the module boundary answers it, exactly as §4's ruling said it would.

**F2 — does `packRegistry.ts` collapse into the manifest loader, or into `editionSignature.ts`?** **Not yet, and the reason is directional.** A manifest is inert data that must be certified BEFORE it becomes a row, and certification asks the pack's functions what they do (§4.2 perturbs the real signature function). So the manifest layer depends on the registry; folding the registry into it would make the thing being checked import the thing checking it. The collapse becomes possible when `signature_fn_ref` is resolved at boot from a ROW rather than from a literal map — 030 §5.2's end state — which needs a loader that reads the database, and that is a bead of its own. **The two halves are kept honest by a contract test rather than by care:** `[...MANIFESTS.keys()]` must equal `REGISTERED_VERTICALS`, so a pack cannot exist without a manifest and a manifest cannot name a pack that does not exist. (v1.0.0 wrote that assertion `Object.keys(MANIFESTS)`, which was true of the object literal this record shipped; **E04-D07** made both registries `Map`s so an inherited key cannot resolve, and the expression moved with them. The SET compared, the contract test that asserts it and this F2 ruling are untouched — see the 006 row for E04-D07.)

**A third structural choice, recorded because a reviewer would otherwise ask.** The manifests are three FLAT files in `src/catalog/`, not a `manifests/` subdirectory. `tests/contract/catalog-surface.test.ts` asserts that every catalog sibling is re-exported from `index.ts` and that no catalog file reaches outside the module, and **both checks read the directory as a flat list** — a subdirectory would have passed the leaf rule while being invisible to the orphan rule, which is a file inside `catalog` that `index.ts` need never mention. The first draft of this bead used a subdirectory and the leaf rule caught it; flattening was the honest fix rather than widening the rule.

## 7. Decision E — A pack is registered by an operator, as DATA

**There is no migration, and that is 030 §3.2's reason 1 rather than a shortcut:** *"'Add a vertical' must not mean 'change the database.'"* The tables are shipped with text keys, so registering a pack is an INSERT. 049 §6.5 already proved the card path against a real database *"without one line of DDL"*; putting a pack row in a migration would make every future vertical a deploy, a rollback plan and a ledger entry — 044 §6's Architecture gate and §8's expand/contract lint, ledger checksum, prior-snapshot upgrade test and compatible-rollback obligation all attach to a migration file and to nothing else, which is the exact per-vertical cost option (a) was chosen to avoid. **The integration suite asserts the migration ledger (`schema_migrations`) is unchanged by the three registrations**, because "we did not add DDL" is the claim and a claim is not evidence.

**And it is an operator script, not a seed at boot.** Two reasons, the second load-bearing:

1. **The application role is REFUSED, by a check and not by a convention.** E02-D06 gives migrations `MIGRATE_DATABASE_URL` and the application `DATABASE_URL`, and the server refuses to boot on a connection whose role owns an append-only table. **v1.0.0 stopped there and was wrong to**: `resolveMigrateUrl` FALLS BACK to `DATABASE_URL` outside production (`scripts/migrateUrl.ts:22-40`, deliberately and loudly), and `pnpm grant-app-role` gives the app role `INSERT` on every table — so a script that only resolved the URL would have seeded the pack rows AS THE APP ROLE and printed "pack registered", producing rows indistinguishable from the owner's. v1.0.1 adds **`assertSchemaOwnerOrThrow`** (`src/services/roleSeparation.ts`), the boot assertion read from the other end: it refuses a connection that owns NONE of the declared append-only tables, before the transaction opens. A boot seed inside the server could never pass it, because the serving connection must FAIL it by construction — which is what makes this reason true rather than aspirational. A superuser is allowed, because a single-role local checkout is a convenience and what a superuser threatens is the serving connection, not a one-shot seed.
2. **Registration is not authorisation, and a seed would blur it.** A boot seed inserting every shipped manifest would register two card verticals in production on the next deploy, silently, as a side effect of shipping code that only proves the core is not comic-shaped. So **`--vertical` is required and has no default**: registering a card vertical is a typed act by a person who meant it, and the script prints the E19-B06 sentence on every run.

The script is **idempotent** (an unchanged re-run is a no-op that says so), supports **`--dry-run`** (which writes nothing, proved on a fresh database), refuses the app role, refuses a vertical no manifest ships for, refuses an uncertified manifest before it opens a transaction, refuses a `signatureFnRef` that does not exist on disk, refuses a re-registration under a different `vertical_code`, and chains `vertical_pack_version.supersedes_id` so a pack's version history is a chain and never a fork.

## 8. Decision F — The card ladder drops MINT and GEM

030 §5.4 sketched `["POOR","GOOD","VG","EX","NM","MINT","GEM"]`. **021 gained B21 on 2026-09-04** — *"'Mint' as a Longbox band"* — **after §5.4 was written.** B21's stated ground is two-part (`021:43`): the word is **not on 037 §2.1's seven-word ladder**, and the trade itself declines to define it — *"'Mint' is a standard that is too difficult to define. All comics have some flaws"*, **which 021 attributes to Mile High Comics and cites at 037 §2.0.5**, not to §2.1. v1.0.0 hung the quote on §2.1, which is the ladder and not the source.

So `certifyConditionSchema` refuses any band folding to MINT or GEM, **in every vertical**, and the two card manifests ship the five bands that survive. **This is not the authoritative card vocabulary:** 049 §8 says *"That is **E08's** and a pack manifest's"* (`049:242`) — the epic, not a bead — so the pointer here is **E08 (E08-B02 per 014 §8 / 015, `longbox-e5b.8.2`)**, and these five are the largest prefix of §5.4's sketch that 021 permits. The comic ladder is untouched — it never contained either word.

**A related line, stated because it looks like a violation and is not.** The card `graderLabelMap` keys are strings like `"PSA 10"`. B17's carve-out is exact: *"the only numbers permitted near a condition are quoted from a named grader's own label"*. The number is in the KEY, quoting the grader; the VALUE is a band from our ladder. Nothing numeric travels through, and `GRADER_MAP_OUTSIDE_LADDER` is what keeps that true.
