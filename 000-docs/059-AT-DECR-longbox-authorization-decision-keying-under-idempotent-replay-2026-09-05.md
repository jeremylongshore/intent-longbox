## 3. Decision A — the ratio is **N:1**, and it is CORRECT rather than tolerated

> **One row is one AUTHORIZATION: one EVALUATION OF THE GRANTS, triggered by a request that reached the decision point. It is not one effect, not one act and not one client intention. A replayed `Idempotency-Key` is a second authorization, because the grants were read a second time, at a later instant, and answered a second time.**

*(The lead says **evaluation** rather than **request** on the data-model lens's H4. The two are one-to-one today and the distinction is not pedantry: it names what the row is a fact ABOUT, so that a future surface which evaluates the grants twice for one request — or once for two — is measured against the fact rather than against today's coincidence.)*

054 §4.5 accepted N:1 as *a documented property* — a cost to be honest about while a later bead decided whether to remove it. This record rules that there is nothing to remove.

**And it is a STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCE, not a designed interlock (K1).** Nobody chose this ratio. It falls out of **where the two mechanisms live in the request lifecycle**: authorization is decided in a Fastify `onRequest` hook (`hook.ts:132`, `:323`) which has no visibility into idempotency state at all, and idempotency is detected inside handler code the hook cannot see (`idempotency.ts:137`, the first statement of the request transaction). A replayed request is therefore re-authorized **before** any stored-response short-circuit can fire, and the second row records a real, independently-taken decision rather than a copy of the first. Saying this plainly matters for one reason above all: **a property that follows from a layout can stop being true when the layout moves**, which is why §9 makes either boundary moving a trigger to re-open this record rather than a refactor somebody notices afterwards.

**The argument is 041 §2.1's, applied without an exception.** *A thing that happened is a row.* When a client retries, the hook does not skip anything: it re-reads the memberships (`hook.ts:296-310`), re-evaluates `authorize()` against the clock, and reaches a verdict that could differ from the first one — because a grant can be revoked, a role can change, a location scope can move and a break-glass window can close between two attempts of the same key. A table that recorded the first answer and swallowed the second would be recording *a decision that was taken twice* as though it had been taken once, and would do so **precisely in the case where the two answers differ** (§6.2). That is not tidying a ratio; it is discarding the more interesting row.

**And the count a reader wants is answerable in principle — but NOT by a join that exists today (K2, and the correction is the lens's).** v1.0.0 wrote that *"how many effects"* is *"a question `request_idempotency` answers"*, which reads as though somebody could go and ask it. They cannot: **there is no join key between the two tables.** `request_idempotency` carries `(shop_id, idempotency_key, route, request_hash, response_*)` and **no `session_chain_id`, no `membership_id` and no decision reference** (`migrations/009:47-77`); `authorization_decision` carries no key of the request (§4, deliberately). The two tables meet on `shop_id` and a timestamp, and **matching rows on time is a guess** — which 042 §5.4 refuses by name for the neighbouring mechanism.

So the honest statement, and it is the one that goes in front of anybody who plans work on this table: **the effect count is answerable in principle from the effect side alone — `request_idempotency` knows how many distinct acts a shop performed — and the mapping from a decision row to its effect is NOT reconstructible today.** *What happened to the book* is answered by the domain rows, which carry `operator_id` and `actor_verified` (048 §6.3); *under what authority* is answered here. **Nothing joins the two.** An investigator or a retention design that assumes otherwise finds out during an incident rather than during a review, which is why §11 routes the sentence to **E03-B09** rather than leaving it in a record only this bead's reader opens.

**One wording repair falls out, and it belongs in 054 rather than here.** 054 §4.2 closes with *"four ATTEMPTS, not four requests"*. The noun is inverted: a replay **is** a request, so four rows are four requests; what four rows are not is four **acts**. The ruling did not move and nothing that depends on it moved, so it is amended into 054 as a patch row with the original left verbatim (054 v1.1.3).

## 4. Decision B — the dedup key may **never** be a column, and four names are refused by the closed list

> **`authorization_decision` never gains `idempotency_key`, `request_id`, `attempt_no` or `effect_id`, under those or any other spelling. The closed column list refuses them by name, beside the eleven 054 §4.2 already refuses.**

**`idempotency_key` is a JOIN KEY, and it joins to the request's own body.** This is the argument that closes option A independently of every other one. `request_idempotency` is keyed `(shop_id, idempotency_key)` and carries `route`, `request_hash` and **`response_body`** (`migrations/009:47-77`). A decision row carrying the key is therefore one join away from the request that produced it and from the response it returned — and the response of `POST …/scan-sessions` is the scan session's identifier, which is the book. 054 §4.2 refuses `correlation_id` **by name** for exactly this reason: it *"would make every row joinable to the request's own item"*. An idempotency key is the same join under a different name, and it is worse in one respect — a correlation id is minted by the server, while this one is minted by the client and is therefore also a value an operator's phone chooses.

**A digest of the key does not help, and the reason is worth writing down.** The plaintext sits in `request_idempotency`, so any reader holding both tables can hash the plaintext and complete the join; a deterministic digest of a value stored in clear beside it is not a protection, it is a formality. Only a keyed MAC under a secret the reader lacks would break the join — and the reader this table is defended against is the same process that would hold the secret. So there is no hashed variant of option A that survives this section.

**`attempt_no` — the shape the bead names as (C) — is refused on four grounds, none of them privacy.** It is not a join key and the argument above does not reach it; these do. **(i) It needs a read the hook does not have.** Deriving it means `SELECT`ing `request_idempotency` at `onRequest`, which adds a round trip to the hot path that 054 §10 already books as a cost — *"every mutating request now costs one extra round trip"* — and doubles it. **(ii) It is wrong exactly under concurrency.** The first attempt's row is INSERTed inside its transaction and is invisible until commit (042 §5.3's whole construction), so two genuinely concurrent attempts of one key both read *absent* and both record `1`. A counter that is right when nothing is happening and wrong when something is is worse than no counter, because it will be believed. **(iii) It is a COUNT about a person's client**, on a table whose migration refuses `duration_ms` and `count` by name (`migrations/028`; 054 §4.2 property 3). **(iv) It has no defined value in the case that matters** — after a rolled-back first attempt there is no earlier row to count from, so the request that actually did the work would also record `1` (§6.2).

**`request_id` and `effect_id` are refused pre-emptively**, because they are the same object under names a future author would not think to check against this section: a per-request identifier is 054 §4.2's `correlation_id`, and an identifier of the effect is the domain row, which is the item.

**What replaces the key, since something must.** Nothing needs to. The question *"is this decision one of several for one effect?"* is deliberately not answerable from this table alone — the same posture 054 §4.2 takes toward every other per-person question here — and, per K2 above, it is **not answerable by a join today either**. That is a stated gap with an owner (§11, E03-B09), not a capability this section quietly assumes.

> **WHAT THIS SECTION DOES *NOT* REFUSE, said explicitly so no later bead reads it as a blocker (H5).** It refuses a **STORED COLUMN**. It does **not** refuse a **QUERY-TIME derived ordinal** — an investigator's or a scheduled job's query that orders this table's rows within a window and calls the second one a second attempt, computed at the moment of asking and written nowhere. The two are different objects: a column is a standing affordance that every row carries and every reader inherits, while a query is an act somebody takes with cause, which is the same distinction 054 §13 draws in refusing to re-add the chain index while keeping the join. **E11-B09's break-glass read path and E13-B04-D1's detector tooling may derive whatever they can compute**; what they may not do is persist it here, or present the result as a count of ACTS (§5). If such a query later needs a key to be exact rather than heuristic, that is an amendment to this record with its own argument — not a column added because a report wanted one.

## 5. Decision C — no collapsing view, no `ON CONFLICT`, and every reader reports **DECISIONS**

> **There is no view over `authorization_decision` and none may be added. The writer's INSERT carries no `ON CONFLICT` clause and the table carries no `UNIQUE` constraint. Every reader of the table reports its rows as DECISIONS or AUTHORIZATIONS, and no reader, DTO, report or sentence may present a row count as a count of requests, acts or effects.**

**Why not the view (option B).** A view that collapsed replays would need one of two things, and both are refused elsewhere in this system. It would need the key — which §4 refuses — or it would need to guess, by grouping rows on `(shop_id, membership_id, route_path, permission, decision)` inside a time window and calling the group one act. That guess is a fuzzy match, and 042 §5.4 pins the opposite for the neighbouring mechanism in as many words: *"No fuzzy comparison, ever… a hash whose matching rule is negotiable is a hash whose meaning drifts."* A read model that guesses which rows are the same act is worse than a documented ratio, because the guess arrives wearing a view's name and a reader stops asking.

**And a collapsing view would be the wrong artifact even if it were exact** (§6.3): a de-duplicated one-row-per-act table is closer to the per-operator activity log 022 P3 forbids than the noisy authorization list it would replace.

**Why not `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` on the pool.** This is the cheapest form of option A and the one 054 §13 under-described — it needs no move into the request transaction and, taken on the pool, its unique index is held for the length of a micro-transaction rather than of a request (§6.1c). It is refused on §6.2's ground rather than on lock order, and on two more: it silently swallows a second decision whose verdict differs from the first, and it would leave refusals on safe methods un-deduplicated anyway, since a `GET` carries no key and `NULL`s are distinct in a Postgres unique index by default. It would make the table 1:1 exactly where the domain row already attributes the act, and leave it N:1 exactly where this table is the only witness.

**What the readers say instead.** The two existing readers are `unreconciledBreakGlassSessions`, which does not touch this table at all, and `decisionsByUnreconciledSessions`, which does. The second is corrected by this bead (§7): its projection is named `decisions`, and its prose says *authorizations*, never *acts*.

**AND THE VOCABULARY IS A BOUNDARY RATHER THAN A HABIT (H2/H3, the data-model lens's condition on its acceptance).** v1.0.0 carried this rule as a field name plus a regex over source text, and the lens's objection is the one this record makes about itself elsewhere: *"a convention decays exactly where this record predicts it will."* Two things change:

1. **The reader has a named exported return type — `AuthorizationDecisionCount`** (`src/services/auth/authorizationAudit.ts`, re-exported from the module's `index.ts`) — whose **only numeric field is `decisions`**. A type nobody can name is a boundary nobody can be held to, so it is exported rather than inlined.
2. **`pnpm arch` gains **rule 3d**, on rule 3b's precedent** (`checkAuthorizationDecisionCountNouns`): in any file under `src/` **or `scripts/`** that reads or names this table, a **declared numeric field** called `acts`, `effects` or `requests` is a red build. `scripts/` is in scope because a CLI that printed *"three acts"* would be exactly as wrong as a service that returned it. The matcher is anchored on `: number`, so it catches the **type** and never the prose — this record, the module and the rule itself all discuss the nouns at length, and a checker that counted discussion would teach the next author to stop explaining themselves.

**The regex over source text is KEPT as belt** (§8 I4). Two mechanisms with different blind spots: the type stops a field from being declared, the source scan stops one from being returned through an anonymous shape. Neither is redundant with the other, and the negative direction of the arch rule is exercised by fixture — a rule that has never failed is indistinguishable from one that cannot.

## 6. The four consequences, answered one at a time

### 6.1 Lock order (042 §5.3(b))

The declared order is **`request_idempotency` INSERT → `app_session` FOR NO KEY UPDATE → `scan_session` anchor**, in every mutating handler, always, enforced by the I22 lint rather than by review. Three facts, and the first settles it.

**(a) There is no request transaction to move the write INTO.** The decision is taken in an `onRequest` hook (E1), which runs before any handler and therefore before `beginIdempotency` (E3). *"Move the write inside the transaction"* is not a small change of position: it means deferring the record of an authorization until after the handler has started, and **a REFUSED request never reaches a handler at all**. Refusals are the class of row this table most exists for — 019 T24 counts them and 054 §4.3 records them on every method — so an audit written inside the request transaction could not record the majority of what it is for. That is a structural refusal, not a preference.

**(b) A UNIQUE taken in the hook would add a fourth lock AHEAD of the three, taken by a different unit of work from the one that owns the order.** If the write were keyed and inside the request transaction, the acquisition order would become *audit UNIQUE → idempotency INSERT → session → anchor*, and a replay would block on the in-flight original's audit row **for the whole duration of the original request** — duplicating the serialization point 042 §5.3 already gets from `UNIQUE (shop_id, idempotency_key)`, and adding the audit table to the hot path's deadlock surface. 042 §5.3(b) exists to make a deadlock *unconstructible rather than rare*; a lock acquired in a hook that fires before the handler which owns the order is precisely the shape it refuses.

**(c) The honest concession: the `ON CONFLICT`-on-the-pool variant does NOT breach the lock order**, and 054 §13's one-line description of this option — *"it moves the write into the transaction §4.5 deliberately keeps it out of"* — is true only of the variant that wants the decision and the effect to commit together. The pool variant keeps the write outside, holds the unique index for one micro-transaction, and costs the hot path nothing new. It is refused on §6.2, not here — and saying so is what stops the next reader believing the lock order did all the work.

### 6.2 What a rolled-back request leaves behind

054 §4.5 states the tear and its direction: *"a decision row can commit for a request whose work then fails. The reverse cannot happen."* That direction is **correct for an audit of authority**, and it survives only while the write is unkeyed.

Put a key on it and the direction inverts in the case 042 §5.3 step 4 describes explicitly — *"If the first rolls back, the second's insert succeeds and it does the work. Also correct — nothing happened, so nothing should be replayed."* Now trace the audit: the first attempt's decision row **committed on the pool** before its transaction rolled back, so it holds the key; the second attempt — the one that actually performs the act — reaches `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` and **records nothing**. The table then names an authorization for a request that did nothing, and is silent about the request that did something.

**And it fails worst when the two decisions differ.** A membership revoked between the two attempts, a role changed during a handover (034 §2.7's *"a role change is two rows"*), a break-glass window closing, an operator session moved to another location: the second decision names a different grant, a different role, or is a refusal. `DO NOTHING` discards it. **A keyed audit discards precisely the row an investigation is looking for** — the one that is not the same as the one before it.

### 6.3 022 P3 — does collapsing replays make a person more or less reconstructible?

**More.** A one-row-per-effect table is a clean list of acts; the noisy list is not. Under N:1 a row count is not an act count, and a reader who joins through the membership — the join 034 §3.3's accessor gates and 019 T35 governs — still cannot get from this table to *"what this person did on this shift"* without a correction they cannot compute from these columns. Collapsing would remove that friction and deliver, in one artifact, the per-operator activity list 022 P3's CFO constraint refuses: *"the cheapest way to satisfy T35 is never to build a per-operator surface, and no such surface may be built and then restricted."*

**This is stated as a consequence and NOT claimed as a control.** Noise is not a control; an attacker, or an over-eager report author, is not stopped by an inconvenient ratio. The controls remain the ones 054 §4.2 names — a closed column list, no route, no DTO, no index on either per-person join — and this record's contribution to them is §4, which keeps the join key out. 054 §0's discipline applies unchanged: an absence asserted by a test is stronger than an absence argued in prose, and §8 puts as much of this as it can into tests.

### 6.4 T35(c) and E03-D14's reconciliation reads

**The reconciliation proper has no stake in the keying.** `unreconciledBreakGlassSessions` runs `app_session` → `membership` → `membership_revocation` and touches `authorization_decision` **not at all** — which is the same fact that exposed the false justification for the index 054 F1 removed. Keying or not keying this table changes nothing about which sessions fail to reconcile.

**The companion read has one stake, and it is vocabulary rather than cardinality.** `decisionsByUnreconciledSessions` answers *"did these flagged chains reach a decision, and on what"*. Presence — *did this chain act at all* — is identical under N:1 and 1:1. Only the COUNT differs, and the count is the thing the module currently mislabels (E7). §7 repairs it.

**E03-D14 changes the population, never the cardinality.** The Longbox-origin predicate widens *which* sessions are in scope; it does not make one request into two rows or two into one. So E03-D14 inherits nothing from this decision except the corrected noun.

## 7. What this bead changes in the tree, and the DEFECT it found

> **It was a DEFECT, and this record does not get to call it a naming preference (K3).** The builder's superseded §2.1 draft framed K1 as *"never a defect, only a mislabelled field"*; the lens rejected that framing in its own statement — *"the harm there was a false sentence about cardinality sitting in shipped code … not a downgrade of the finding to a naming preference."* **A shipped security-audit surface whose one counting reader described its number with a noun the record had withdrawn is a defect in that surface**, whatever the schema underneath it does. **The defect is K1's harm sitting in the code rather than in a record.** `decisionsByUnreconciledSessions`' header claims the result is *"enough to say 'this session was allowed two privileged acts'"* (`authorizationAudit.ts:242` at `720e80c`, `:321` at the rebase base). Under the property 054 §4.5 documents on the page before, two rows may be **one act replayed**. So the one reader of this table that produces a number describes that number with the exact noun the record withdrew. A bead that rules *the ratio stands* owes the fix to the place the ratio is misread.

The change is small and entirely mechanical:

- the projection is renamed `count(*)::int AS n` → `count(*)::int AS decisions`, and the returned field `count` → `decisions`, so a caller cannot read *acts* out of a field named for them;
- the return shape becomes the named exported type **`AuthorizationDecisionCount`**, and **`pnpm arch` rule 3d** refuses the wrong noun anywhere under `src/` or `scripts/` that reads this table (§5, H2/H3) — so the correction is a boundary and not a one-time edit;
- the header says **authorizations, never acts**, and cites this record;
- `recordAuthorizationDecision` gains the rule as a comment where the next author will meet it: one row per arriving request, no `ON CONFLICT`, no key column;
- the contract test gains §8 I1–I4;
- 054 I9a's integration case gains a sentence naming this record as the one that decided to keep it, so a future reader finds the ruling from the test.

**Nothing else in `src/` moves, no migration is written, and `migrations/028` is not edited** (044 §3: a landed migration is immutable, and the enforcement lives in the test that reads it).

## 9. Alternatives considered

**A1 — Key on `(session_chain_id, route_template, idempotency_key)` with a `UNIQUE` and `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`.** Refused on four independent grounds, any one of which is sufficient: it requires the join key §4 refuses; it discards the second decision exactly when it differs from the first (§6.2); inside the transaction it cannot record refusals at all (§6.1a) and adds a fourth lock ahead of the declared order (§6.1b); and it leaves keyless refusals N:1 regardless, so it fixes the half that did not need fixing.

**A2 — The same, keyed on `(shop_id, idempotency_key)` as 054 §13 phrased it.** Refused for A1's reasons and one more: a `UNIQUE` over a **client-choosable** value on a policied table is the shape 056 §6.3 rules on. Tenant-prefixing satisfies that rule, which is why the shape is stated there as shop-prefixed; it does not touch the rest.

**A3 — An `authorization_decision_effect` view that collapses replays, plus a read-model rule that every reader goes through it (option B).** Refused: the view needs either the forbidden column or a fuzzy grouping over grant, route and time — and 042 §5.4 refuses fuzzy matching by name for the neighbouring mechanism. A guess wearing a view's name is worse than a documented ratio (§5).

**A4 — `attempt_no` derived from `request_idempotency` (option C as the bead phrased it).** Refused on §4's four grounds: an extra round trip on the hot path, a value that is wrong exactly under concurrency, a count on a table that refuses counts, and no defined value after a rolled-back first attempt.

**A5 — Store a keyed MAC of the idempotency key instead of the key.** Refused: it breaks the join only for a reader who lacks the secret, and the reader this table is defended against is the process that holds it (§4).

**A6 — Move the audit write into the request transaction unkeyed, so a rolled-back request records nothing.** Refused, and it is the mirror of the whole design: 054 §4.5 rules that *the decision is a fact about the REQUEST, not about the write*, a refused request has no transaction to join, and 041 §4.5's `40001` retry budget would make every retried transaction rewrite its decision. That is the ratio problem again, one layer down and worse.

**What would have to be true for §3 to be wrong — and the list is a RE-OPEN TRIGGER, not a curiosity (K1).** §3's property is a **structural consequence** of where the two mechanisms sit in the request lifecycle, so it stops holding the moment either boundary moves. **Any of the following re-opens this record before the change lands:**

1. **A replay stops re-evaluating the grants** — the hook reads a cached verdict, or authorization moves behind the idempotency replay so a settled key short-circuits before the decision is taken. Then the second row records a decision nobody took, and it is a duplicate rather than a fact.
2. **Idempotency detection moves INTO a hook** (or anywhere upstream of `enforcePermission`), so the authorization site can see that a request is a replay. The ratio would then be a CHOICE rather than a consequence, and a choice has to be argued rather than inherited.
3. **Authorization moves INTO a handler**, inside the request transaction. §6.1's structural refusal — a refused request never reaches a handler — would no longer describe the system, and both the recording rule and the lock order would need re-deriving.
4. **A second evaluation of the grants happens for one request**, or one evaluation serves two — which is why §3's lead names the EVALUATION rather than the request (H4).

None of these is the design today (E1, E3). A bead that proposes one amends this section **first**, in the same PR, rather than discovering afterwards that a refactor silently changed what an audit table's rows mean.

## 10. Consequences

**What gets better.**
- The one place the tree misread its own ratio is fixed (§7), and the field a reader would have quoted is named for what it counts.
- The 1:1 shape can no longer arrive by accident: three absences — a column, a constraint, a view — are now checked rather than argued (§8).
- 054 §8's E03-D15 bullet is discharged, and nothing about the accessor module, the retention window or the reconciliation cadence moved.

**What gets worse, stated plainly.**
- **The table still grows faster than the effects it records, and now PERMANENTLY rather than pending a fix.** A retrying client and E05-B08's offline queue both produce more rows than acts, and the growth bound remains the shop's `ordinary` rate token, taken before the write (054 §4.3, F4). **Retention is E03-B09's and this record does not invent a window.**
- ⚠ **E03-B09 IS ELEVATED FROM A RESIDUAL TO A NAMED PRE-VOLUME GATE (the data-model lens's most-costly item), and K5 is what it must be told.** *No retention window, sweep, rollup or aggregate over `authorization_decision` may assume one row per act.* Two facts compound: the multiplier is **permanent** (§3), and E05-B08's offline queue makes a replay **the normal case rather than the pathological one** (042 §2.2, §5.6) — a counter that queues a shift's writes and replays them produces the ratio by design. A rollup that summed rows as acts would over-report a shop's activity, and a window sized on *"requests per day"* would be sized on the wrong number. **And it cannot correct for the multiplier by joining, because there is no join key** (§3, K2). So E03-B09 designs against DECISIONS, or it designs against a number that does not mean what it says.
  - **Why a GATE and not a note.** The failure is not one a review catches: it is a table sized for one volume meeting another, and a rollup nobody re-derives once a report has been built on it. The gate sits **before volume** — before this table carries more than a pilot's traffic — and **not** before this bead closes.
- **Anybody counting rows must be told what they are counting.** The vocabulary rule (§5) is carried by one contract assertion and by prose everywhere else, which is weaker than a schema constraint and is exactly the kind of rule that decays. It decays into an over-count of activity, never into an under-count — which is the safer direction for an audit and the wrong direction for a report about a person, and 022 P3 governs the second.
- **This record makes an argument where a constraint would have been cheaper to trust.** 041 §4.2(i) prefers a constraint wherever one exists; here one exists and is refused, on the ground that the rule it would enforce is not true. That is the correct reason to refuse one, and it is still a departure from the preference order, so it is written down rather than absorbed.
- **The integration lane did not run for this bead** (§0). Four invariants are proved without a database and one is inherited un-re-run; the PR's required check is what closes that, and no sentence here should be read as though it already had.
